## Stage 1.2 – Planet & World Design

### Step 1: Decide the World Type

There are three options:

**Option A — Exact Earth (Not Recommended)**

* Same countries
* Same roads
* Same cities
* Huge amount of data
* Difficult to optimize
* Some map imagery has licensing restrictions

**Option B — Earth Inspired (⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Recommended)**

* Similar continents and climates
* Original cities and countries
* Original roads and landmarks
* Much easier to expand
* Avoids copyright and licensing issues

**Option C — Completely Fictional**

* Unlimited creativity
* Doesn't feel like the real world

For your game, I recommend **Option B**.

---

# Step 2: Use Real Earth Data as Reference

We'll use public and open geographic data as inspiration, not copy commercial map imagery.

Free resources include:

* **Google Earth** for visual reference and planning (follow its licensing terms; don't copy or redistribute imagery directly). ([LinuxLinks][1])
* **OpenStreetMap (OSM)** for roads, buildings, rivers, and points of interest under its open license. ([GitHub][2])
* **Natural Earth** for free public-domain continent, coastline, river, and terrain datasets. ([30 Day Map Challenge][3])

# Step 7: World Scale

Rather than building the entire planet immediately, we'll divide the world into **streamed map regions**. Many open-world games load only nearby areas to reduce memory usage. ([The Verge][4])

Example progression:

* Version 0.1: One city
* Version 0.5: One region
* Version 1.0: One country
* Later: Multiple continents

This makes the project achievable for a solo creator.
