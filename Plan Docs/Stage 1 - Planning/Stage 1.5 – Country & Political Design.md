# Stage 1.5 – Country & Political Design

## Goal

Create a believable political world where:

* Countries have unique identities.
* Borders make geographic sense.
* Governments affect gameplay.
* Laws differ between countries.
* Travel, trade, and jobs feel realistic.

---

# Step 1 – World Structure

We'll organize the world like this:

```text
Planet
 ├── 8 Continents
 │
 ├── 72 Countries (target over time)
 │
 ├── 450+ States / Provinces
 │
 ├── 5,000+ Cities (long-term vision)
 │
 ├── 50,000+ Towns & Villages
```

For the first playable version, you'll only build **one country with one city**. Everything else stays as future expansion.

---

# Step 3 – Government Types

To create variety, countries can have different systems such as:

* Republic
* Constitutional monarchy
* Federal republic
* Parliamentary democracy
* Island federation

Different governments can influence taxes, policing, and public services in gameplay.

---

# Step 4 – Administrative Levels

Each country can be divided into:

* States / Provinces
* Districts
* Cities
* Towns
* Villages

This hierarchy supports local governments, police jurisdictions, and regional economies.

---

# Step 5 – Economy

Every country should have strengths.

Possible sectors:

* Agriculture
* Technology
* Tourism
* Manufacturing
* Fishing
* Mining
* Renewable energy
* Finance
* Logistics

These industries create jobs and trade routes.

---

# Step 6 – Transportation Policy

Plan national infrastructure:

* Highways
* Railways
* Airports
* Seaports
* Bus terminals
* Metro systems (large cities)
* Bicycle paths

This helps shape city growth and travel gameplay.

---

# Step 7 – Culture

Each country will define:

* Languages
* Festivals
* Traditional foods
* Architecture
* Clothing styles
* Music
* Sports
* Local customs

This makes travel feel meaningful instead of every place looking the same.

---

# Step 8 – Laws & Gameplay

We'll keep laws appropriate for a broad audience and use them to add gameplay depth.

Examples include:

* Traffic regulations
* Speed limits
* Parking rules
* Fishing permits
* Wildlife protection
* Building permits
* Business licenses

Breaking laws can lead to fines or other in-game consequences.

---

# Step 9 – Public Services

Every developed country can eventually include:

* Hospitals
* Police stations
* Fire stations
* Schools
* Universities
* Courts
* Parks
* Libraries
* Postal services
* Public transportation

These become important mission locations and daily-life destinations.

---

# Step 10 – Free Resources for Research

We'll use free, legal sources for inspiration:

* **OpenStreetMap** – Road networks, city layouts, transport ideas.
* **Natural Earth** – Public-domain country boundaries and geography.
* **GeoNames** – Open geographic database for place naming inspiration.
* **Wikipedia** – Background research on geography, government structures, and cultures (verify important facts with official sources if needed).
* **NASA Earth Observatory** – Climate and environmental references.

---

# Step 11 – Long-Term Expansion Plan

Instead of creating dozens of countries immediately:

* **Version 0.1:** One original country
* **Version 0.3:** Three regions
* **Version 0.5:** One complete nation
* **Version 1.0:** Two neighboring countries
* **Future updates:** Additional continents and countries

This staged approach keeps the project achievable for a solo developer.

## Before Stage 1.6

We should make one key design decision that will affect the entire game:

**Should the game use:**

1. **A realistic scale** (cities and travel distances similar to the real world, requiring many years to build), or
2. **A compressed scale** (cities and roads feel realistic but are shortened, similar to many successful open-world games), making the project much more practical for a solo creator.

For a no-budget solo project, I recommend the **compressed scale**, because it provides the feeling of a vast world while keeping development manageable. This decision will guide how we design cities, roads, vehicles, and travel in the next stage.
